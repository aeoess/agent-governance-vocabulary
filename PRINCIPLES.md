# Shared principles

Draft, started in [#177](https://github.com/aeoess/agent-governance-vocabulary/issues/177). Change it by pull request, like any other file here.

Each project keeps control of its own code, roadmap, license, architecture and business. What is decided together is only the work created as a group, including these principles, interoperability rules, attribution and other common artifacts. Shared decisions do not change another project's ownership, licenses, business or independent revenue.

1. Each project stays independent and keeps control of its own work.
2. The common layer is governed together, openly, with no single project as the permanent gatekeeper.
3. There is no required central protocol. Projects connect at clear boundaries while keeping their own architecture.
4. Verification used in shared work states what was checked, who checked it, what the evidence supports and what it does not establish. For each conclusion it states whether the checked party could forge or suppress evidence the conclusion depends on, and whether the events were captured as they happened or reconstructed afterward. A conclusion that depends on evidence the checked party could forge or suppress is not presented as independently observed, and one that depends on reconstruction is not presented as observed as it happened. Unknown information stays unknown. Verification does not imply endorsement of the project.
5. Claims stay at their layer and their subject. Whatever result categories a project uses, a weaker result should not be collapsed into a stronger conclusion, and a claim about a tool, an agent or an organization is not a claim about another.
6. Attribution and provenance travel with the work.
7. Participation is open, voluntary and not exclusive. There is no fee to be listed or to join, and pilot costs or paid work are agreed separately by the projects involved. Being listed on the map, joining a pilot, or reviewing or contributing technical work does not enroll a project in shared governance or in economic decisions. Each needs the project's own explicit opt-in. Taking part in economic decisions is not agreeing to any resulting arrangement, which needs a separate agreement by the parties involved. Silence, past collaboration and continued technical participation never count as consent. Joining one pilot creates no obligation to join another.
8. We support each other's work where it makes sense, including review, distribution, integrations and discovery.
9. Decisions about the shared layer should follow a public process. When we need a group decision, we can vote.
10. If shared work creates economic value, the projects that opted in to economic decisions should agree openly how any shared proceeds are used or shared. Those decisions must account for the projects and people whose work the value depends on, including those that did not opt in, and no one is left out only for not taking part in the decision. This creates no automatic right to payment, and each project keeps its own business and the revenue from its independent work. We don't need to design that now.
